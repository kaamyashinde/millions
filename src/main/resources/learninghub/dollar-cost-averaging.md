## Summary
Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals — regardless of whether the market is up or down. It removes the pressure of trying to time the market and helps smooth out the effect of price swings.

## Key Concepts
- **Dollar-cost averaging (DCA)**: Investing the same fixed amount at regular intervals (weekly, monthly, etc.) rather than all at once.
- **Lump-sum investing**: Investing a large amount all at once, as opposed to DCA.
- **Market timing**: Trying to predict the best moment to buy or sell. Most investors — including professionals — fail to do this consistently.
- **Average cost per share**: With DCA, you automatically buy more shares when prices are low and fewer when prices are high, which can lower your average purchase price over time.
- **Automation**: Most investment platforms let you set up automatic recurring investments, making DCA easy to maintain without thinking about it.

## Explanation
One of the most paralysing questions for new investors is: "Is now a good time to invest?" The market might feel too high. Or it might be falling and you're scared it'll fall further. Either way, you wait — and often end up not investing at all.

Dollar-cost averaging sidesteps this entirely. Instead of trying to find the perfect moment, you commit to investing a set amount — say, 1,000 NOK — on the first of every month, no matter what. Some months you'll buy when prices are low and get more shares for your money. Some months you'll buy when prices are high and get fewer. Over time, the average price you paid tends to sit below the market's peak, because you automatically picked up extra shares during dips.

The emotional benefit is just as real as the mathematical one. Markets are volatile — they go through periods of sharp decline that feel alarming. DCA keeps you investing through those rough patches automatically, rather than freezing up or panic-selling. As [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/d/dollarcostaveraging.asp) notes, consistent investing through downturns is one of the habits that separates long-term wealth builders from those who repeatedly buy high and sell low.

There is an honest counterpoint worth knowing: research has shown that lump-sum investing (putting all your money in at once) tends to outperform DCA about two-thirds of the time over long periods, simply because markets tend to rise over time, so earlier investment usually wins. But lump-sum investing requires both having a large sum available *and* the psychological courage to deploy it all at once. For most people — investing regular income as it arrives — DCA is not just practical, it's optimal.

Norwegian investment platforms like [Nordnet](https://www.nordnet.no/magasinet/investering/maanedssparing/) offer a feature called *månedssparing* (monthly saving), which lets you automate DCA into index funds or stocks with no manual effort each month.

## Example
You invest 1,000 NOK per month in a global index fund over 4 months:

- Month 1: price = 100 NOK per unit → you buy 10 units
- Month 2: price = 80 NOK (market dips) → you buy 12.5 units
- Month 3: price = 90 NOK → you buy 11.1 units
- Month 4: price = 110 NOK → you buy 9.1 units

You invested 4,000 NOK total and own 42.7 units. Your average cost per unit is 4,000 ÷ 42.7 = about 93.7 NOK. The current price is 110 NOK, so your portfolio is worth about 4,697 NOK — a 17.4% gain, even though the market only rose 10% from your starting point. The dip in month 2 worked *for* you because you kept investing.
